# Translation terminology

The Simplified Chinese, German and Japanese translations cover interface text,
anatomical names and the existing clinical explanations. Translation does not
change vessel geometry, simulated parameters, clinical probabilities or cited
study results. Conventional identifiers such as MCA, ICA, ACA, PCA, PICA, AICA,
SCA, MLF and NIHSS remain conventional identifiers.

## Simplified Chinese (zh-CN)

The reference is the National Committee for Terms in Sciences and Technologies
(全国科学技术名词审定委员会), *人体解剖学名词*, second edition. The committee's
[human anatomy terminology catalogue](https://www.cnterm.cn/sd/yxjk/zwh/rtjp/)
identifies the terminology programme. The committee-authored
[2013 text mirrored by Fudan University](https://xtjp.fudan.edu.cn/Upload/Files/201804100314393640155.pdf)
provides the accessible terminology entries used for this translation. Its title
pages contain unfinished publishing metadata, including placeholder ISBN data;
it is treated as a committee-authored terminology manuscript rather than proof
of the final printed book's bibliographic details.

Examples of explicit regional terminology changes include 颈内动脉, 大脑中动脉,
脑桥, 丘脑, 脉络丛前动脉, 辐射冠, 内侧丘系, 伏隔核, 面神经 and 视辐射.
The explanations use Simplified Chinese prose with these anatomical names;
they are translations of the application's existing educational explanations,
not definitions copied from the terminology book. Syndrome eponyms, study names
and model assumptions are retained where appropriate.

Clinical Chinese wording also follows established mainland usage; for example,
同向偏盲 is used for homonymous hemianopia, as in the
[Beijing Municipal Health Commission's stroke information](https://wjw.beijing.gov.cn/bmfw_20143/jkzs/jbzs/201912/t20191217_1250088.html).
This clinical usage reference is distinct from the committee's anatomical
terminology catalogue.

## German (de)

Anatomical naming follows established German neuroanatomical usage, including
the Latin vessel names customary in German medical texts: A. carotis interna,
A. cerebri media, A. communicans anterior, A. basilaris and the cerebellar
arteries. The source checked is Thieme's
[*Klinische Neuroanatomie – kranielle MRT und CT*, chapter on cerebral arteries and vascular territories](https://shop.thieme.de/api/media-proxy?url=https%3A%2F%2Fnginx%2Fmedia%2F18%2F19%2F9a%2F1653305463%2F9783131634146_musterseite.pdf%3Fts%3D1653305463).
The translation uses German prose and established clinical names such as
Dysarthrie, Hemianopsie, Neglect and internukleäre Ophthalmoplegie. Shared Latin
anatomical names, for example Putamen or Hippocampus, intentionally remain the
same spelling as the English entry.

## Japanese (ja)

Anatomical names follow the Japanese Association of Anatomists'
[*解剖学用語 改訂13版*, publicly available terminology](https://www.anatomy.or.jp/yougo.html),
specifically its [vascular terminology](https://www.anatomy.or.jp/file/pdf/yougo/kaibo6.pdf)
and [nervous-system terminology](https://www.anatomy.or.jp/file/pdf/yougo/kaibo7.pdf).
Examples include 内頸動脈, 中大脳動脈, 前交通動脈, 脳底動脈, 後下小脳動脈,
中心前回, 内包, 視床 and 内側毛帯. Clinical explanations are translated into
Japanese prose; conventional abbreviations and eponyms remain recognizable.

## Scope of verification

Completeness tests check every authored localized key, including dynamic text
templates. Selected terminology assertions guard regional anatomical names and
unchanged conventional abbreviations. These checks establish translation
coverage and terminology consistency; they are not independent clinician review
of all educational prose. The roadmap's clinical review remains a separate task.


## Consciousness terminology

Neurologic English *stupor* is translated as **昏睡** in Simplified Chinese,
following the [MSD Chinese explanation](https://www.msdmanuals.cn/home/brain-spinal-cord-and-nerve-disorders/coma-and-impaired-consciousness/stupor-and-coma): strong stimulation can briefly awaken the patient; **昏迷** denotes coma.
German uses **Sopor**, the quantitative consciousness level in the
[AWMF 022-016 classification table](https://register.awmf.org/assets/guidelines/022-016l_S1_Akute_Bewusstseinsstoerung_jenseits_der_Neugeborenenperiode_2021-01-abgelaufen.pdf).
That archived guideline is cited for nomenclature only, not treatment advice.
Japanese uses **昏迷** for stupor and **昏睡** for coma, as distinguished in
[Igaku-Shoin's neurological examination article](https://www.igaku-shoin.co.jp/misc/medicina/shinkei4601/).
These locale corrections do not change the model's consciousness thresholds.
